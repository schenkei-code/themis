# TEIL A: STRAFRECHT

## A1. Österreichisches Strafrecht (StGB AT)

### Körperverletzung
- **§ 83 StGB AT**: Leichte Körperverletzung — bis 1 Jahr oder Geldstrafe. Privatanklagedelikt (du musst selbst klagen)
- **§ 84 StGB AT**: Schwere Körperverletzung (>24 Tage Gesundheitsschädigung, berufliche Beeinträchtigung, Werkzeug) — bis 3 Jahre
- **§ 85 StGB AT**: Körperverletzung mit schweren Dauerfolgen (Lähmung, Entstellung) — 1–10 Jahre
- **§ 86 StGB AT**: Körperverletzung mit tödlichem Ausgang — 1–10 Jahre
- **§ 87 StGB AT**: Absichtlich schwere Körperverletzung — 1–5 Jahre

### Gegen die Ehre
- **§ 111 StGB AT**: Üble Nachrede — unwahre Tatsachen behaupten. Privatanklagedelikt. Bis 6 Monate
- **§ 113 StGB AT**: Vorwurf einer bereits abgetanen strafbaren Handlung
- **§ 115 StGB AT**: Beleidigung — Herabsetzen in der öffentlichen Meinung. Bis 3 Monate
- **§ 116 StGB AT**: Herabsetzung staatlicher Symbole
- **Praxis**: Bei Beleidigung/übler Nachrede → Privatanklage beim zuständigen Bezirksgericht; Frist: 6 Wochen ab Kenntnis

### Cyber-Delikte & Stalking
- **§ 107a StGB AT**: Beharrliche Verfolgung (Stalking) — körperlich, digital, durch Dritte. Bis 1 Jahr (Privatanklagedelikt)
- **§ 107b StGB AT**: Fortgesetzte Gewaltausübung — 6 Monate bis 5 Jahre
- **§ 107c StGB AT**: Cybermobbing — Verletzung der persönlichen Lebenssphäre via Netz. Bis 1 Jahr
- **§ 118a StGB AT**: Widerrechtlicher Zugriff auf ein Computersystem (Hacking) — bis 2 Jahre
- **§ 119 StGB AT**: Verletzung des Briefgeheimnisses
- **§ 119a StGB AT**: Missbräuchliches Abfangen von Daten — bis 1 Jahr
- **§ 126a StGB AT**: Datenbeschädigung — bis 2 Jahre
- **§ 126b StGB AT**: Störung der Funktionsfähigkeit eines Computersystems (DDoS) — bis 3 Jahre

### Diebstahl, Betrug, Vermögensdelikte
- **§ 127 StGB AT**: Diebstahl — bis 6 Monate oder Geldstrafe
- **§ 128 StGB AT**: Schwerer Diebstahl (>5.000€, Einbruch, Waffe) — 6 Monate bis 3 Jahre
- **§ 129 StGB AT**: Diebstahl durch Einbruch — bis 5 Jahre
- **§ 146 StGB AT**: Betrug — Täuschung + Vermögensschaden. Bis 6 Monate (leicht), bis 3 Jahre (>5.000€)
- **§ 147 StGB AT**: Schwerer Betrug (>50.000€) — 1–10 Jahre
- **§ 148 StGB AT**: Gewerbsmäßiger Betrug — bis 3 Jahre
- **§ 153 StGB AT**: Untreue — bis 3 Jahre
- **§ 156 StGB AT**: Betrügerische Krida — 1–10 Jahre
- **§ 164 StGB AT**: Hehlerei — bis 3 Jahre

### Sexualdelikte
- **§ 201 StGB AT**: Vergewaltigung — 1–10 Jahre; bei Folge schwere Körperverletzung bis 15 Jahre
- **§ 202 StGB AT**: Geschlechtliche Nötigung — 6 Monate bis 5 Jahre
- **§ 205 StGB AT**: Sexueller Missbrauch wehrloser oder psychisch beeinträchtigter Personen
- **§ 206 StGB AT**: Schwerer sexueller Missbrauch von Unmündigen (unter 14) — 1–10 Jahre
- **§ 207a StGB AT**: Pornografische Darstellungen Minderjähriger (Besitz/Verbreitung) — bis 3 Jahre

### Nötigung, Drohung, Freiheitsdelikte
- **§ 99 StGB AT**: Freiheitsentziehung — bis 3 Jahre
- **§ 105 StGB AT**: Nötigung — bis 1 Jahr
- **§ 106 StGB AT**: Schwere Nötigung (Selbstmorddrohung, schwere Körperverletzung angedroht) — bis 5 Jahre
- **§ 107 StGB AT**: Gefährliche Drohung — bis 1 Jahr (öffentlich oder gegen Familie bis 2 Jahre)

### Sucht & Drogen (SMG)
- **§ 27 SMG AT**: Unerlaubter Umgang mit Suchtgiften (Besitz/Kauf/Besitz zum Eigenbedarf) — bis 1 Jahr; gering = Diversion möglich
- **§ 28 SMG AT**: Vorbereitung von Suchtgifthandel (>5-fache Grenzmenge) — bis 3 Jahre
- **§ 28a SMG AT**: Suchtgifthandel (über Schwellenmenge) — 1–15 Jahre je nach Menge

### Verfahrensrecht AT
- **§ 48 StPO AT**: Unschuldsvermutung
- **§ 49 StPO AT**: Recht auf Verteidiger, Recht zu schweigen
- **§ 164 StPO AT**: Vernehmung — Recht zur Aussageverweigerung
- **§ 174 ff. StPO AT**: Festnahme und Verhaftung — Vorführung binnen 48h zu Richter
- **§ 193 StPO AT**: Einstellung des Verfahrens
- **§ 200 StPO AT**: Diversion — Geldbuße, gemeinnützige Leistung, Probezeit statt Verurteilung
- **§ 488 StPO AT**: Privatanklage — Frist 6 Wochen, Kostenvorschuss ~160€

# TEIL O: US-RECHT (FEDERAL & KEY STATE LAW)

## O1. US Constitutional Rights (Bill of Rights)

- **1st Amendment**: Freedom of speech, press, religion, assembly, petition — government cannot restrict. Private platforms (Meta, TikTok, YouTube) are NOT bound by 1st Amendment — they can remove content freely (Section 230)
- **4th Amendment**: Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures — police need a warrant (probable cause). Applies to digital data: *Carpenter v. United States* (2018) — cell phone location data requires warrant
- **5th Amendment**: Right to remain silent (Miranda), protection against self-incrimination, due process, no double jeopardy. "Pleading the 5th" = refusing to answer to avoid self-incrimination
- **6th Amendment**: Right to a speedy trial, right to an attorney (public defender if can't afford), right to confront witnesses
- **14th Amendment § 1**: Equal Protection + Due Process for all persons (not just citizens)

## O2. US Criminal Law (Federal)

### Federal Criminal Code (18 U.S.C.)
- **18 U.S.C. § 1030**: Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) — unauthorized computer access. Up to 10 years. Used against hackers, scraping without permission
- **18 U.S.C. § 1343**: Wire Fraud — scheme to defraud using electronic communications. Up to 20 years
- **18 U.S.C. § 1341**: Mail Fraud — up to 20 years
- **18 U.S.C. § 2261A**: Interstate Stalking — up to 5 years; cyberstalking via electronic means
- **18 U.S.C. § 875**: Interstate threats via communications (emails, DMs threatening harm) — up to 5 years
- **18 U.S.C. § 2252**: Child pornography — mandatory minimums 5–20 years
- **18 U.S.C. § 1956**: Money laundering — up to 20 years
- **18 U.S.C. § 1962**: RICO Act — organized crime, pattern of racketeering. Up to 20 years
- **18 U.S.C. § 3553**: Federal sentencing guidelines

### Miranda Rights (Miranda v. Arizona, 1966)
- Police MUST read before custodial interrogation: "You have the right to remain silent. Anything you say can and will be used against you. You have the right to an attorney..."
- If not read → statements may be suppressed

### Drug Law (Controlled Substances Act, 21 U.S.C.)
- **21 U.S.C. § 841**: Distribution/manufacturing — Schedule I (heroin, MDMA): 5–40 years; Schedule II (cocaine, meth): same
- **21 U.S.C. § 844**: Simple possession — up to 1 year (federal); state laws vary widely (some states decriminalized marijuana)
- **21 U.S.C. § 812**: Schedules I–V — marijuana still Schedule I federally despite state legalizations

## O3. US Civil Law & Contracts

### Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
- **UCC § 2-201**: Statute of Frauds — contracts over $500 must be in writing
- **UCC § 2-313**: Express warranties
- **UCC § 2-314**: Implied warranty of merchantability
- **UCC § 2-315**: Implied warranty of fitness for particular purpose
- **UCC § 2-607**: Buyer must notify seller of breach within reasonable time

### Contract Law (Common Law)
- **Offer + Acceptance + Consideration** = binding contract (no writing needed for contracts under $500)
- **Consideration**: each party must give something of value
- **Statute of Frauds**: real estate, contracts >1 year, marriage agreements must be written
- **Promissory Estoppel** (Restatement § 90): promises relied upon to one's detriment are enforceable even without consideration
- **Unconscionability** (UCC § 2-302): court can void contracts that are shockingly unfair
- **Breach of contract remedies**: compensatory damages, consequential damages, specific performance, liquidated damages

### Tort Law
- **Negligence**: Duty + Breach + Causation + Damages (*Palsgraf v. Long Island Railroad*)
- **Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress (IIED)**: extreme/outrageous conduct causing severe emotional distress
- **Defamation**: false statement of fact, published to third party, damages (public figures need to prove *actual malice* — *NYT v. Sullivan*, 1964)
- **Invasion of Privacy**: 4 torts — intrusion, public disclosure of private facts, false light, appropriation of name/likeness
- **Strict Liability**: manufacturer liable for defective products regardless of fault (*Greenman v. Yuba Power*)

## O4. US Employment Law

- **Title VII (42 U.S.C. § 2000e)**: Discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin — applies to employers with 15+ employees
- **Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA, 29 U.S.C. § 623)**: Protects workers 40+ from age discrimination
- **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA, 42 U.S.C. § 12101)**: Reasonable accommodations required
- **Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA, 29 U.S.C. § 201)**: Federal minimum wage ($7.25/hr federal, states vary), overtime at 1.5x after 40h/week
- **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA, 29 U.S.C. § 2601)**: 12 weeks unpaid leave per year for birth, adoption, serious illness — job protected
- **National Labor Relations Act (NLRA, 29 U.S.C. § 151)**: Right to organize, join unions, collective bargaining
- **At-Will Employment**: Most US states — employer can terminate for any reason without notice (exceptions: discrimination, retaliation, implied contract)
- **WARN Act (29 U.S.C. § 2101)**: 60 days notice required for mass layoffs (100+ employees)
- **Worker Classification**: Employee vs. Independent Contractor — IRS 20-factor test; misclassification = back taxes + penalties

## O5. US Intellectual Property

### Copyright (17 U.S.C.)
- **17 U.S.C. § 101**: Definitions — "work made for hire" owned by employer
- **17 U.S.C. § 102**: Copyright protects original works of authorship fixed in tangible medium (automatic from creation)
- **17 U.S.C. § 106**: Exclusive rights — reproduction, distribution, public display, derivative works
- **17 U.S.C. § 107**: Fair Use — 4 factors: purpose (commercial?), nature of work, amount used, market effect
- **17 U.S.C. § 512 (DMCA)**: Safe harbor for platforms; takedown notices; counter-notices
- **17 U.S.C. § 1201**: Anti-circumvention (DMCA) — bypassing DRM is illegal
- Duration: Life + 70 years (post-1977 works); 95 years from publication (pre-1978 corporate works)
- Registration: Copyright.gov — not required but necessary to sue for statutory damages ($750–$150,000/work)

### Trademark (15 U.S.C. — Lanham Act)
- **15 U.S.C. § 1114**: Infringement of registered marks
- **15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)**: False designation of origin — trade dress, unregistered marks
- **15 U.S.C. § 1125(c)**: Trademark dilution — famous marks
- **15 U.S.C. § 1116**: Injunctive relief
- **15 U.S.C. § 1117**: Damages — defendant's profits, plaintiff's damages, treble damages for counterfeiting
- Registration: USPTO (United States Patent and Trademark Office) — $250–$350/class
- **®** = registered; **™** = unregistered claim

### Patent (35 U.S.C.)
- **35 U.S.C. § 101**: Patentable subject matter — process, machine, manufacture, composition
- **35 U.S.C. § 102**: Novelty requirement
- **35 U.S.C. § 103**: Non-obviousness requirement
- Duration: 20 years from filing date
- Utility patent: $800–$3,000+ filing; Design patent: ~$500

## O6. US Privacy & Internet Law

### Federal Privacy Laws
- **COPPA (15 U.S.C. § 6501)**: Children's Online Privacy Protection Act — under 13: parental consent required, no behavioral advertising
- **HIPAA (45 C.F.R.)**: Health data protection — applies to healthcare providers, insurers, business associates
- **ECPA (18 U.S.C. § 2510)**: Electronic Communications Privacy Act — wiretapping, stored communications
- **VPPA (18 U.S.C. § 2710)**: Video Privacy Protection Act — video viewing history; used against streaming services sharing data
- **FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45)**: FTC enforces unfair/deceptive practices — includes data breaches, privacy violations

### State Privacy Laws
- **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA / CPRA, Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100)**: Right to know, delete, opt-out of data sale; applies to businesses with $25M+ revenue or 100K+ consumers
- **Virginia CDPA**: Similar to CCPA, effective 2023
- **Colorado CPA, Connecticut CTDPA**: Similar frameworks
- **California "Shine the Light" Law (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.83)**: Right to know if data shared with third parties for marketing

### Section 230 (47 U.S.C. § 230)
- **§ 230(c)(1)**: Platforms NOT liable for third-party content — protects YouTube, Instagram, TikTok
- **§ 230(c)(2)**: Platforms NOT liable for good-faith content moderation
- Exceptions: federal criminal law, sex trafficking (FOSTA-SESTA), intellectual property

### DMCA Safe Harbor (17 U.S.C. § 512)
- Platforms protected from copyright liability if they: have no knowledge, no financial benefit from infringement, respond to takedowns
- **DMCA Takedown Notice** must include: identification of work, identification of infringing material, contact info, good faith statement, perjury statement, signature
- Counter-Notice: 10–14 days to respond; if filed, platform must restore content after 10–14 business days unless rightsholder sues

## O7. FTC Rules for Influencers & Advertising

- **FTC Act § 5 (15 U.S.C. § 45)**: Unfair or deceptive acts or practices — basis for all FTC influencer enforcement
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 C.F.R. Part 255)** (updated 2023):
  - Must clearly disclose material connections (payment, free products, family/friend/employee relationships)
  - "Ad", "#ad", "Paid partnership" at the BEGINNING of post (not buried in hashtags)
  - Disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous" — same language as the post
  - Applies to: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, Twitter/X, podcasts, blogs
  - Applies even for: free products, discounts, affiliate links, family relationships
- **FTC Penalties**: Up to $51,744 per violation (2024 rate); companies + influencers both liable
- **Specific Platform Rules**:
  - YouTube: must check "paid promotion" box + verbal disclosure in first 30 seconds
  - TikTok: Branded Content toggle + "Ad" label
  - Instagram: "Paid partnership" label + "#ad" in caption
  - Podcasts: verbal disclosure at beginning of episode
- **New 2023 Rules**: AI-generated content that is deceptive = FTC violation; fake reviews = $50,000/review fine

## O8. US Family Law (Federal + Key State)

### Federal
- **Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act (UCCJEA)**: Which state has jurisdiction over custody (adopted by all 50 states)
- **Parental Kidnapping Prevention Act (28 U.S.C. § 1738A)**: Federal enforcement of custody orders across states
- **Child Support Enforcement (42 U.S.C. § 651)**: Federal framework; each state has guidelines (income shares model or % of income)

### Key State Principles (vary by state)
- **Divorce**: No-fault divorce available in all 50 states; waiting periods vary (6 months CA, 1 year NY)
- **Property Division**: Community property states (CA, TX, AZ, NV, WA — 50/50 split) vs. equitable distribution states (most states — "fair" not necessarily equal)
- **Alimony/Spousal Support**: Discretionary, based on length of marriage, earning capacity, standard of living
- **Child Custody**: Best interests of the child standard — courts consider stability, parent-child relationship, each parent's ability
- **Child Support**: Federal guidelines through state formulas; typically 17–25% of non-custodial parent's income for 1–3 children

## O9. US Business & Corporate Law

- **Delaware LLC Act / Delaware General Corporation Law**: Most startups incorporate in Delaware — flexible, court of Chancery, predictable law
- **S-Corp**: Pass-through taxation, max 100 shareholders, US citizens only
- **C-Corp**: Double taxation but needed for VC investment, stock options (ISOs), IPO
- **LLC**: Most flexible — operating agreement governs, pass-through tax by default
- **Sole Proprietorship**: No filing needed, full personal liability
- **Partnership**: 2+ persons, joint and several liability unless LLP
- **Piercing the Corporate Veil**: Court ignores LLC/Corp protection if: commingling funds, fraud, undercapitalization, alter ego
- **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)**: Mutual or one-way; reasonable scope/duration required to be enforceable (CA: very limited enforcement of NDAs)
- **Non-Compete Agreements**: Enforceable in most states (narrowly); **California**: virtually unenforceable (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600); FTC proposed nationwide ban (2024, ongoing legal challenge)

## O10. US Bankruptcy (11 U.S.C.)

- **Chapter 7**: Liquidation — most assets sold, eligible debts discharged. Means test required. Takes ~3–6 months
- **Chapter 13**: Reorganization for individuals — repayment plan 3–5 years, keep assets. For those with regular income
- **Chapter 11**: Business reorganization — restructure debts, continue operating
- **Automatic Stay (11 U.S.C. § 362)**: Filing immediately stops all collection actions, lawsuits, foreclosures
- **Exempt Property**: Varies by state — federal exemptions include $27,900 homestead, $4,450 vehicle, tools of trade
- **Non-Dischargeable Debts**: Student loans (except undue hardship), child support, alimony, recent taxes, fraud-related debts
- **Means Test (11 U.S.C. § 707(b))**: Income must be below state median to qualify for Chapter 7

## O11. US Immigration (8 U.S.C.)

- **8 U.S.C. § 1101**: Definitions — immigrant vs. non-immigrant visa
- **H-1B Visa**: Specialty occupation workers — annual cap 65,000 (+20,000 advanced degree); employer-sponsored; lottery
- **O-1 Visa**: Extraordinary ability in arts, science, business, athletics — no cap, merit-based
- **EB-1/EB-2/EB-3**: Employment-based green cards — priority workers, advanced degrees, skilled workers
- **DACA**: Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals — not a law, executive policy; ongoing legal challenges
- **Deportation/Removal (8 U.S.C. § 1227)**: Grounds include: criminal convictions (aggravated felony, drugs), visa overstay, document fraud
- **Asylum (8 U.S.C. § 1158)**: Must apply within 1 year of arrival; persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, social group
